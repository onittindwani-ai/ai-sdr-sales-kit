# 06. The agent does the whole job, so who needs you?

**They say:** "Salesforce says its agent works deals for weeks and months. Doesn't that replace all of this?"

## What they really mean

They read or heard the launch language, and they want to know what is left for a human to do.

## Say it out loud

It does sound that way. Salesforce says Hunter works a pipeline from research to outreach, collaborating with sellers over weeks and months. Listen to that last part, collaborating with sellers. It needs sellers to work with, and its page says reps set the goal and can require approval. So the job doesn't go away, it moves to whoever plays the rep. In your business, who would that be? With {{agency_name}}, that's my job, and {{approval_step}} before anything goes out.

## Send it by email

Subject: Does the agent replace the whole job?

Hi,

Salesforce describes Hunter as collaborating with sellers over weeks and months, and says reps set the goal and can require approval.

So I read it as a strong helper for people who already sell. Someone still sets the goal, sets the rules and reviews what goes out.

At {{agency_name}}, that someone is me. What you see at the end is {{outcome}}, and along the way, {{approval_step}}.

Who would play that part on your side if you went with the agent?

{{your_name}}

## Ask this next

If that agent started tomorrow, whose calendar would the weekly check go on?

## When the tool is the better fit

If they have a sales leader or experienced reps who can set goals and review the agent's work, the agent gives them leverage you cannot match inside their own Salesforce. In that case, tell them to pilot it when they can.

## Facts this answer uses

Salesforce's announcement and outbound agent page (sources 1 and 2), checked 2026-11-09. "It moves to whoever plays the rep" is an opinion based on that wording. Recheck the pages on the day you use this.

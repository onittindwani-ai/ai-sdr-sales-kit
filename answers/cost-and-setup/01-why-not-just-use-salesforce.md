# 01. Why not just use Salesforce?

**They say:** "Salesforce has its own AI sales agent now. Why would I pay a small agency?"

## What they really mean

They heard a big name and they do not want to overpay. Most of the time they have not looked at what it takes to run that agent, so they are checking whether you know.

## Say it out loud

That's a fair question, and if you already ran Salesforce with a sales team, I'd tell you to look at it seriously. Salesforce says its agent, Hunter, works under rules a sales team sets, and its own page says reps set the goal and keep full control. So it's made for a company that already has reps. You don't have that yet, and what you want is {{outcome}}, so what you'd buy from me is the result, with {{agency_name}} doing the running.

## Send it by email

Subject: Salesforce, and whether you need me at all

Hi,

Good question, and I would rather answer it straight. Salesforce says its agent, Hunter, works under rules a sales team sets, and that reps set the goal and keep full control. That suits a company with reps and a Salesforce setup.

You told me you have neither right now, and what you want is {{outcome}}. That is the part {{agency_name}} takes on, from start to finish.

If you do plan to build a sales team soon, tell me and we can look at both options side by side.

{{your_name}}

## Ask this next

Who on your team would set that agent's goals and check its work every week?

## When the tool is the better fit

If they already use Salesforce, have reps who can set goals and approve outreach, and have someone who owns the setup, Hunter may suit them better than you once it is open to them. Say so, and offer to help them decide.

## Facts this answer uses

Salesforce's outbound agent page (source 2), checked 2026-11-09. The view that it suits companies with reps is an opinion based on that wording. Recheck the page on the day you use this.

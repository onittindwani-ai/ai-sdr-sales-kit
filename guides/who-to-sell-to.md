# Who to sell to: the founder with no sales team

If you are starting an AI SDR agency (a business that uses AI to find sales prospects and book calls for clients), the most useful decision you will make is who you refuse to sell to. This guide helps you make it.

## The idea in plain words

Big companies are now shipping their own AI sales agents. Salesforce describes its agent, Hunter, as working under rules a sales team sets, and says reps set the goal and keep full control (source 2). Apollo's announcement says it was built to help sales teams grow revenue faster (source 3). As of 2026-11-09, Salesforce's latest announcement said pilot, with general availability planned for November 2026 (source 1).

Read that wording and you can see who it is made for: a company that already has Salesforce and already has reps. **That is our opinion, based on that wording, and not something Salesforce has said.**

A founder with no sales team and no Salesforce has a different problem. That founder does not want another tool to run. They want booked calls. That founder is who this kit helps you sell to.

## The 8 signs of a good client

Score each one 0 (no), 1 (partly) or 2 (yes). You will find most of this out on the discovery call.

| # | Sign | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The founder or owner still does the selling | Ask who did the last 3 sales |
| 2 | There is no sales team to run a tool | Ask who would check the work each week |
| 3 | They do not run Salesforce, or run it only lightly | Ask what their CRM is and who looks after it |
| 4 | They sell to other businesses | Look at their customer list or website |
| 5 | One new customer is worth enough to pay for your service | Ask what a typical customer is worth to them |
| 6 | They can name who they want to reach | Ask them to describe their best customer |
| 7 | They can take more work and deliver it well | Ask how many new customers they could handle now |
| 8 | They will show up for the calls you book | Ask who takes them and how soon |

**How to read your score.** 13 or more out of 16: a good fit, go ahead. 9 to 12: find out what is missing before you offer anything. 8 or less: be honest and say it is not a fit yet. These cut offs are a starting point we chose, not a measured result. Change them once you have your own clients to learn from.

If signs 2 and 8 score 0, stop, whatever the total says.

## Who to walk away from

| Who | Why | What to tell them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A company with Salesforce, reps and someone who owns the setup | Hunter may suit them better once it is open to them | "You have what that agent needs. Look at it first." |
| A HubSpot customer with someone who has weekly time | HubSpot's agent may be enough, at $1 for each lead it recommends outreach for (source 5) | "Check whether you already have this before spending with me." |
| A founder who enjoys building and has the hours | They will be happier running a tool | "Try it yourself first. I can point you the right way." |
| A business with no clear offer | Outreach cannot fix an offer nobody wants | "Let's get the offer right before you pay for outreach." |
| Someone who wants a promised number of sales | You cannot honestly promise it | "I can't promise a number. If you need that, I'm not the right fit." |
| A heavily regulated business with no legal review | Too much risk for both of you | "Please involve your lawyer or compliance person first." |

## How to find these founders

You do not need a giant list. You need a short list of people who fit. Good places to look:

1. **Your own background.** The trade you know best is the fastest to learn and the easiest to talk to.
2. **Trade directories and local business groups** for one type of business in one area.
3. **Company websites that show the founder is still the contact**, such as "call me" or a founder's own email.
4. **Job posts for a first sales hire**, because they show the founder has the problem and has budget in mind.
5. **Referrals from people who sell to the same founders**, such as accountants and web designers.

Pick one niche and stay with it long enough to learn it. Every answer in this kit gets stronger when it speaks that niche's language.

## Stay within the rules

- LinkedIn's User Agreement (effective 3 November 2025) bars bots and unauthorised automation for adding contacts, sending messages or engaging, and its help page bars third party software that scrapes or automates activity (sources 8 and 9). Read both before you build any LinkedIn step.
- Email and phone outreach rules differ by country. Check the rules where you and your prospects are based. This guide is not legal advice.
- Anyone who says stop is stopped, straight away, on every channel.

## Your first message to this founder

Written as a starting point. Fill in the brackets by hand for each person, and only write what you have actually checked.

> Hi [first name], I noticed [one real and specific thing about their business]. It looks like you are still the one bringing in new customers. I help [your niche] get booked calls without having to hire a sales team or run another piece of software. Not sure if that is useful to you right now. Would it be worth a short chat?

What makes this work is the real and specific thing. If you cannot find one, do not send the message.

## What not to say

- Do not say that Salesforce or any other company is bad. Say who their product suits.
- Do not say Hunter has launched unless you read that on Salesforce's own page today.
- Do not state a price for Hunter. We found none published.
- Do not quote results you have not produced yourself.
- Do not say a tool "cannot" do something unless the vendor says so. Say what you do and let them compare.
